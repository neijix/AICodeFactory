# Generic tells and what to do instead

A tell is a choice made by default rather than for this product. One tell is rarely fatal;
three or four together are what makes a reader think "template" or "generated" within a
second. Use this list to critique screenshots, not markup.

Every entry has the same shape: what it looks like, why it reads as generic, what to do
instead. "Instead" is a starting point, not a new default: pick from the brief.

## Whole-page looks

### Cream and terracotta

- **Looks like:** warm off-white background, rust or terracotta accent, a serif headline,
  muted olive or sand secondary colours.
- **Why generic:** it became the shorthand for "warm, human, crafted" and is now applied to
  products that are none of those.
- **Instead:** take the warmth from the scene (materials, photographs, the product's own
  colours). If the product really is warm and crafted, pick an accent from its world (a glaze,
  a fabric, a wood) and check it against the four colour strategies.

### Night mode with neon

- **Looks like:** near-black page, glowing cyan, magenta or lime accents, blurred colour blobs
  behind glassy translucent cards.
- **Why generic:** dark-with-glow is the default for "technical" or "AI", so it signals the
  category, not the product, and fails the competitor test at once.
- **Instead:** decide light or dark from the reader's scene. If dark is right, make it a
  calm dark: one accent at moderate chroma, no glow, surfaces separated by lightness steps.

### Faux-editorial

- **Looks like:** thin rules everywhere, numbered sections, small monospaced uppercase labels
  ("01 / FEATURES") on every block, wide margins, a serif display face.
- **Why generic:** it borrows the look of a magazine without having a magazine's content.
  The labels become decoration that every section wears.
- **Instead:** keep rules and labels only where they organise real content (an index, a
  spec sheet, a long read). One labelling system, used sparingly, in the text face.

### Faux-brutalist

- **Looks like:** flat saturated fills, thick black borders, hard offset shadows on cards and
  buttons, chunky sans-serif.
- **Why generic:** the offset shadow is a costume; real brutalist sites are raw in structure
  (default type, exposed grid), not in a shadow recipe.
- **Instead:** if the brand is loud and playful, get it from scale, colour (Committed or
  Drenched strategy) and copy voice. Drop the shadow offsets.

### The template page

- **Looks like:** centred hero with headline, subline and two buttons; logo strip; three
  cards with icons; alternating image-left/image-right rows; testimonial carousel; dark CTA
  band; four-column footer.
- **Why generic:** it is the order every page builder ships with. A reader skims it without
  reading because they know where everything is and expect nothing.
- **Instead:** derive the section order from the argument (claim, evidence, doubts answered,
  action). Vary section shapes by content. Show the product early and large.

### Purple-blue gradients

- **Looks like:** violet-to-blue (or pink-to-violet) gradient on buttons, headline text,
  hero backgrounds or borders.
- **Why generic:** the most common colour decision in generated pages; it marks the page as
  made without a colour decision.
- **Instead:** a flat colour chosen by strategy. If a gradient is needed, keep it within one
  hue (lightness only) or make it depict something (light, sky, material).

## Components

### Icon cards

- **Looks like:** a grid of equal boxes, each with a line icon in a tinted circle, a two-word
  title and two lines of text.
- **Why generic:** much more common on weak pages than strong ones. The icons carry no
  information and the equal boxes say every point matters equally.
- **Instead:** a plain list with strong first words; one feature shown large with a real
  screenshot; or a comparison table. If points differ in weight, make them differ in size.

### Pill badges

- **Looks like:** rounded chips above headlines ("New", "AI-powered", "v2.0 is here"),
  tag clouds of pills.
- **Why generic:** a filler that tries to add urgency or category to a headline that should
  do it itself. Far more frequent on weak pages.
- **Instead:** put the news into the headline or a dated line of text. Use tags only where
  they filter or classify something real.

### Heavy shadows and glass

- **Looks like:** large soft shadows under every card, frosted translucent panels, stacked
  elevation everywhere.
- **Why generic:** depth used as decoration flattens the hierarchy: when everything floats,
  nothing does. Also over-represented on weak pages.
- **Instead:** separate areas with space, a background step or one hairline. Keep elevation
  for things that are actually above the page (a menu, a dialog).

### Rounded everything

- **Looks like:** the same large radius on cards, images, buttons, inputs and sections.
- **Why generic:** a framework default left unchanged.
- **Instead:** choose the radius from the direction (square for precise or editorial,
  small for most, large only when softness is the point) and vary it by element size.

### Testimonial carousel

- **Looks like:** auto-rotating quotes with avatar circles and five stars.
- **Why generic:** readers ignore moving quotes; invented-sounding praise lowers trust.
- **Instead:** one or two real quotes, set large, with full name, role and company, placed
  next to the claim they support. No quotes the user did not supply.

### Stat strip

- **Looks like:** "10k+ users · 99.9% uptime · 4.9 rating" in a row of big numbers.
- **Why generic:** round numbers without a source read as invented, and often are.
- **Instead:** one specific, sourced number placed where it proves the claim. Numbers come
  from the user only.

### Emoji and spot illustrations as section art

- **Looks like:** emoji bullets, isometric people, abstract 3D blobs.
- **Why generic:** they replace the product with stand-ins.
- **Instead:** real screenshots, photographs the user provides, or a diagram that explains
  something specific. Otherwise, let type and space carry the section.

## Type

### Many sizes, small steps

- **Looks like:** six to ten font sizes, each a little bigger than the last; headings that
  barely differ from body text.
- **Why generic:** the opposite of what strong pages tend to do: few sizes, far apart.
- **Instead:** three or four sizes with large ratios between them. See
  `TYPE-AND-PALETTE.md`.

### The fashionable display face

- **Looks like:** whichever display serif or geometric grotesque is everywhere this season.
- **Why generic:** recognisable as the default of the moment; dates the page immediately.
- **Instead:** justify the face: its period, its shapes, the scene, the licence the user
  holds. A well-set system stack beats an unjustified trendy face.

### Gradient or outlined headline text

- **Looks like:** headline words filled with a gradient, or set as outlines, or with one
  word highlighted in a coloured pill.
- **Why generic:** effect in place of a stronger sentence.
- **Instead:** make the sentence specific and the size large. Emphasis through weight or
  colour on at most one phrase.

### Centred long text

- **Looks like:** paragraphs of body text centred under a centred heading.
- **Why generic:** template habit; hard to read beyond two lines.
- **Instead:** left-align body text in a measured column (see measure in
  `TYPE-AND-PALETTE.md`). Centre only short display lines, if at all.

## Copy

### Vague headline

- **Looks like:** "Unlock your potential", "The future of work", "Build faster, together".
- **Why generic:** fits any product; fails the competitor test before design starts.
- **Instead:** what it is, for whom, with one concrete outcome, in the product's own nouns.

### Abstract nouns

- **Looks like:** solutions, experiences, workflows, journeys, ecosystems, seamless.
- **Instead:** the actual objects and actions: invoices, rotas, kilns, deploys, booking a
  table.

### Two equal buttons

- **Looks like:** "Get started" and "Learn more" side by side, same weight.
- **Why generic:** the page has not decided what it wants.
- **Instead:** one primary action with a specific label; a secondary text link only if a
  real second audience exists.

### Lorem ipsum or invented proof

- **Instead:** draft copy written from the argument, marked as draft; placeholders for any
  name, logo, quote or number the user has not given.

## Layout

### Everything centred, everything the same width

- **Instead:** a grid with deliberate asymmetry; let one element break the column (the bold
  move); vary section widths by content.

### Even spacing everywhere

- **Looks like:** identical padding between every section and inside every block.
- **Instead:** group by proximity. Tight inside a group, large between groups, the largest
  gap before the most important section.

### A first screen without the product

- **Looks like:** a headline and an abstract visual; the product appears three scrolls down.
- **Instead:** show the real thing (screenshot, photograph, sample) in the first screen
  unless the scene argues for a slower opening.

## Quick scoring for a screenshot

Count the tells you see. Zero to one: refine details. Two to three: fix those before any
polish. Four or more: the direction is missing; go back to the argument and the scene in
`SKILL.md` rather than patching tells one by one.
